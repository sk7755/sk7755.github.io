Jaehoon Chung

Computational geometry, data structures, and algorithm design

I am a research fellow at the Korea Institute for Advanced Study. My work studies the combinatorial structure of geometric objects — how polygons can be partitioned, covered, and approximated — and turns that structure into algorithms with provable guarantees, including primitives that scientific computing and machine learning rely on.

§ 1

Research

Three strands, connected by the same question: what is the right combinatorial description of a geometric problem, and what does it cost to compute?

Computational geometry

  • Polygon partitioning and covering
  • Containment and approximation of shapes
  • Geometric lower-bound constructions

Algorithmic primitives for AI

  • Distribution-aware nearest neighbours
  • Weighted selection in sorted lists
  • Geometric optimal transport

AI-assisted algorithm design

  • Learning-augmented online scheduling
  • Neural combinatorial optimisation

Current project

Dynamic k-nearest-neighbour search in high-dimensional non-Euclidean geometry

Maintaining exact neighbour queries under insertions and deletions when the underlying metric is not Euclidean, and the dimension is high enough that space partitioning stops paying for itself.
